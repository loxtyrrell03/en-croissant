# Rare-mechanism precision audit: adapter 175 to 176

The six fixed public development puzzles exposed one redundant secondary
heading, not a false tactical win. In eAHH6, **Rg8** already has a specific
Interference explanation: it cuts Re8's defence of Nh8 while attacking Re8.
The additional Double Threat describes the same two original material targets,
same move and same 320-centipawn local certificate. Removing that duplicate
makes the lesson more focused without weakening the proof or changing its
primary explanation.

## Selection and limits

The output-blind selection used the existing tiny 1,679-puzzle public fixture,
its development split only, and the shared evidence-aware sampler. Two cases
were selected in each ordered stratum: interference (eAHH6, fVyR4), deflection
(WhAca, o4FrE), and attraction (UpwI4, SzulO). The seed, complete positions,
source continuations, exclusions and initial hypotheses were saved before any
classifier observations. Prior public-review exclusions include EpYOT, Qq0JW,
all six quiet-trap nominations and reviewed public ordinary-game source groups.
The six source games are mutually disjoint and absent from that recorded review.

This fixture has already supported development. These are fresh relative to
recorded review, not an unseen holdout or six independent accuracy judgments.
Root/full and opposite-colour variants are dependent tests. Source labels only
nominated mechanisms; no source label was treated as adjudicated truth. No
private/owner store, full corpus, network download or runtime was used.

## Independent chess evidence and the overlap boundary

Python-chess independently replays all 12 original/reflected source lines and
closes these finite witnesses:

- **eAHH6:** all 16 legal replies to Rg8 permit a connected material collection
  retaining at least 320 centipawns in the bounded capture/evasion tree. Removing
  h7 permits ...Rxg8+ with no legal recapture, losing the interposing rook.
- **Extra-target control:** adding a black knight on b8 retains the interference,
  but the generic proof now covers Re8, Nb8 and Nh8. After ...Ke3 Rxe8+, every
  legal king evasion allows Rxb8. This is a real additional victim, so Double
  Threat must remain. That branch's gain of at least 820 is not a root minimum.
- **fVyR4:** both legal replies to ...Rh3+, Ke2 and Kf2, block Ra2's defence of
  h2. After ...Rxh2+, every legal check evasion permits ...Rxa2, retaining 600
  centipawns locally. Removing Ra2 removes that specific interference lesson.
- **UpwI4:** every actual defender turn in Ne2+ Kh1 Rxh2+ Kxh2 Rh8# has exactly
  one legal reply. Removing the g2 blocker supplies Kg2 after the final rook
  check, so the named mate no longer applies.

The core change is limited to independently certified overlap. It requires the
same move and ply, the same interposing capturer, a checking-fork entry that
actually collects the interference's defender, exactly the same complete pair
of original material targets, and no larger generic certificate. It does not
establish equality of whole-position evaluations or compare lower bounds as
exact optimal values. Extra targets, different mechanisms and actual later
forks remain separate. The existing Nd7 double threat and castling combination
are not suppressed by a global theme priority.

## Remaining observations

WhAca and o4FrE remain unresolved at their roots; their empty output is not
evidence of positional play. SzulO likewise has no admitted root proof in this
sample, but its later pawn fork and underpromotion stay on their actual plies.
The ply-5 fork is not an immediate tactical lesson and its live label is
**Later: Fork**. It is not reported here as a false root accusation or as proof
that the initial two-rook-sacrifice combination is fully understood.

The independent capture/evasion trees establish finite local material claims,
not best play against every longer quiet plan or whole-game evaluations.
No additional false primary was confirmed within this audit.
