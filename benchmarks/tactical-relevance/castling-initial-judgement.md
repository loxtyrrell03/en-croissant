# Castling context: judgement before fresh output

The 18 positions were selected by SHA ordering from all 71 legal castle options
across twelve already-frozen real game histories. These are candidate options,
not necessarily actual or best moves. No Black queenside castle was eligible;
constructed colour-reflected controls cover that gap, not extra real-game data.

None of these castles gives check. Castling alone is not a tactic. An engine
advantage, a pre-existing pin, or a speculative later capture does not establish
a new root mechanism. I do not assume all eighteen are correct negatives.

## Rare-root review that led here

In ZVq1J, Black starts in check along Qd4-e5-f6-g7. Ne5+ interposes,
but the knight remains pinned to its king and cannot simply take Rd7. The later
Rd2 offer and Qxd2 acceptance change that geometry. Do not borrow the later
discovered check as a proved root fork.

In snAK4, Qd5 threatens Qxg2 mate; after f3, Ra2 traps the queen with Qd5
covering its escape and supporting the rook. The reached trap is distinct from
a complete root proof; Rxe7+ must be included among the legal defences.

In 4Ds65, Bg4 and the later Rae8 concern a pin/preparation. The all-defence audit
must include legal O-O-O, even when that is not the supplied puzzle response.
These root gaps are retained, not relabelled as accuracy successes.
