# Independent dynamic numerical extension

This extension preserves the original six-case `drift-reference.json` and all v3 receipts. Its nine synthetic cases target failure mechanisms that ordinary histories did not cover. It does not evaluate empirical skill, forecast calibration, or likelihood coefficients.

Seven cases use a constant-to-machine-precision win likelihood, so the exact state remains Gaussian and its variance must equal initial variance plus `driftSdYear² * elapsedSeconds / (365.25 * 86400)`. They test a 5.150752-point prior SD at a fractional grid position, a four-hour gap split into one/two/four intervals, near-zero drift, and upper/lower prior centres with twenty years of diffusion. The final twenty-year interval extends beyond the former fixed support. Neither an Euler lattice kernel with matching variance nor a truncated Gaussian transition can satisfy all these analytic distribution checks.

Two cases start at rating zero with prior SD 50, accumulate 300 wins against rating 4000 at one timestamp, and then allow one year of drift. One adds a win and a loss against 4000; the other adds 300 losses against zero. The second case tests recovery through Gaussian transition tails far beyond five or eight transition standard deviations. It ends at mean **−218.578179530**, SD **24.027779250**, and central 95% interval **[−266.396474612, −172.193814004]** under the declared model.

The rare-tail oracle integrates each Gaussian transition directly over the entire source mesh with log-sum-exp and positive trapezoidal weights. It never exponentiates the propagated state or clips transition radius. Its complete posterior is retained in log space. Reported moments/CDFs use separate continuous spline quadrature. Results on a 0.5-point mesh agree with a 1-point mesh within **1.4e−10 rating points**, while a separate domain expansion from [−2000,6000] to [−3000,7000] also converges. Predictive differences are below **6e−15**. The finite meshes and summary tail cutoff are independently varied/diagnosed for these cases; this is numerical evidence for the fixtures, not a proof for every model parameter or history. No FFT-based reference is used for the rare-tail cases.

`verify-performance-dynamic-extensions.py` generates the frozen input/output fixture. `check-performance-dynamic-extensions.mjs` bundles the actual source graph, hashes every compiled TypeScript source, and checks every output point against it. Required errors are below **0.001 rating points** for mean, SD, both interval endpoints and pregame mean, and below **1e−6 absolute probability** for each predicted outcome. It additionally checks exact history-prefix/as-of equality, elapsed-time partition invariance, and the informative near-zero-drift limit. A new dedicated Vitest file repeats these behavioral gates. Receipts refuse overwrite and must be produced against an unchanged source graph.

The frozen `dynamic-extensions-v3-result.json` fails 52 gates. Concentrated-prior mean error reaches 0.1766 points and interval error exceeds two points; twenty-year boundary interval errors reach 199.46 points. The rare-tail reversal has maximum mean error 2385.03 points and predictive error 0.6837. These failures remain retained evidence. The subsequent v4 candidate passes these same numerical gates in `dual-domain-dynamic-v1-result.json`; `DUAL_DOMAIN_INDEPENDENT_AUDIT.md` identifies its source graph, additional checks and limitations. That source acceptance does not establish installed or published delivery.

## Additional chronology and exterior checks

Two current-result tests hold all past games, current opponent and timestamp fixed, then replace only the current result with a win, draw or loss. Their pregame mean and all three forecasts must remain bit-identical. One case has a sharp draw likelihood below exp(−30); another has a remote observed win requiring posterior support expansion. Resolving that observed posterior cannot retrospectively alter its own pregame forecast.

`verify-performance-exterior-risk.py --strong-only --output NEW_FILE` independently integrates two log-concave densities beyond the last trusted interpolation nodes. The frozen strong-curvature fixture is `dynamic-exterior-strong-reference.json`. One density is Gaussian throughout the interpolation interior but has a weaker Gaussian curvature outside, where the first Gaussian quadrature rules have no nodes; the other is a Gaussian prior times a logistic loss likelihood, whose curvature relaxes beyond the edge. The separate older `dynamic-exterior-reference.json` retains a weaker, merely log-concave linear-tail exploration and is not used to justify a finite strong-curvature envelope.

The tests require the analytically propagated exterior envelope to cover the independently integrated outside contribution. They also compare 80 rounds of conservative compaction/diffusion against unmerged Gaussian bounds, check pointwise coverage after likelihood multiplication, and reject a density rising out of the mesh or existing error that prevents proving an interior height. These bounds cover exterior uncertainty only. Interpolation and numerical quadrature still have their separate resolution/convergence tolerances; the tests do not silently turn those approximations into a rigorous all-source error certificate.

Those method-specific envelope tests are retained in `scripts/check-performance-exterior-research.mjs`, which defaults to the archived failed Gaussian-envelope source. The public dynamic Vitest file contains only method-independent output/chronology checks. The extended checker defaults to `--method gaussian-envelopes`; an explicit `--method dual-domain` records the Gaussian-envelope audit as not applicable while retaining every numerical, prefix, partition and current-result gate. Its `numericalGatesPass` field distinguishes those required numerical checks from the method-specific envelope audit. Dual-domain agreement is a numerical convergence check, not a rigorous exterior certificate.
